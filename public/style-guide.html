Writing rules
Five laws you follow on every single story, every platform, every beat.
Rule 01
Lead with the most important fact
The first sentence — the lede — must answer the question a reader would ask: "What happened?" Put the biggest news first. Never bury it three paragraphs in.
Good lede
Kenyan rapper Khaligraph Jones has been announced as the headline act for the 2025 Kakamega Cultural Festival, organizers confirmed Wednesday.
Buried lede
The Kakamega Cultural Festival has been growing every year. This year it will feature many artists. The headline act is Khaligraph Jones.
Rule 02
Every claim needs attribution
You did not witness it. Someone said it, released it, or confirmed it. Always say who. Use: said, confirmed, announced, told Amaica Media, according to.
Attributed
"We want to bring the biggest names in Kenyan music to Western Kenya," festival director Amina Were told Amaica Media.
No attribution
The festival director wants to bring the biggest names in Kenyan music to Western Kenya.
Rule 03
Past tense for events, present for standing facts
Events that happened = past tense. Things still true = present tense.
Correct
The album dropped Friday. It features 12 tracks and runs 47 minutes.
Rule 04
Keep sentences short. One idea per sentence.
Target 20 words per sentence. If a sentence has more than one comma, split it. Readers scan. Make it easy for them.
Rule 05
Full name on first mention, last name after
Write "Sauti Sol" in full on first mention. After that: "the group." For individuals: "Bien Aime Baraza" first, then "Baraza" or "Bien." Apply this rule to every person and act in your story.
Story structure
Entertainment news uses the inverted pyramid. Most important first, background last. Editors cut from the bottom — so protect the top.
What happened Lede — 1 sentence
Who, when, where Context paragraph
Quotes from source 1–2 direct quotes
More detail / reaction Body paragraphs
Background / history Can be cut safely

Quote rule
Use quotes for emotion and opinion. Paraphrase for facts.
Quotes should sound like a real person speaking. Do not quote things you can say more efficiently yourself. Quote when the words themselves matter.
Quote for emotion — correct
"This album is everything I have wanted to say for three years," Nyashinski told Amaica Media.
Useless quote — avoid
"The show will start at 7 PM," the organizer said.

Style & language
Consistent language choices make Amaica Media sound like a professional newsroom, not a blog or a PR sheet.
Attribution verbs — use the right verb for the context
VerbWhen to use
saidNeutral default for most direct quotes
confirmedVerified facts, official statements
told Amaica MediaExclusive or interview quotes — use to mark original reporting
announcedPublic declarations, press releases
according toSourced from another outlet or document
alleged / claimedUnverified information — use carefully, sparingly
Numbers & dates
Spell out one through nine. Use figures for 10 and above.
Write: three songs, 12 tracks, 2,000 fans.
Dates: Saturday, June 14 — not "14/06."
Times: 7 PM — not 19:00, unless in a broadcast schedule.
Titles & names
Capitalize official titles before a name. Lowercase after.
Write: Director Jane Mwangi. But: Jane Mwangi, the festival director.
Song and album titles in quotation marks: "Sura Yako."
Film and show titles in italics where possible, or quotation marks.
Local context
Anchor Kenyan and Western Kenya stories for all readers
One brief identifier on first mention is enough. "Nairobi-based producer Joefreshgoods" or "Bungoma-born comedian Mjango" gives readers orientation without being condescending.
Platform differences — same story, different length
Website article
200–500 words Full inverted pyramid. All 5 Ws. At least one quote. Background at the end.
Radio bulletin
60–80 words Read in under 30 seconds. Lede + two supporting facts + source. No quotes — paraphrase instead.
Social post
Hook + 2–3 lines + CTA No passive voice. One fact per sentence. End with a link or call to action.
Common mistakes
Six things that will make an editor send your story back. Know them before you write.
1
Writing opinions as facts
Wrong
The performance was incredible and the crowd loved every moment.
Fix
The audience sang along throughout the 90-minute set. Organizers said ticket demand exceeded expectations.
2
Vague time references
Wrong
The album dropped recently.
Fix
The album dropped Friday, May 2.
3
Headlines that do not say what happened
Wrong
Big News for Kenyan Music Fans
Fix
Sauti Sol Announces 10-City Kenya Tour Starting July
4
Unattributed quotes
Wrong
"This is the best show we have ever done." — no name, no title.
Fix
"This is the best show we have ever done," said Aisha Omondi, Amaica TV's head of entertainment.
5
Fan blog language in a news article
Wrong
The legendary king of Afrobeats absolutely slayed the stage and left fans shook.
Fix
The headliner drew the largest crowd of the evening. Fans filmed the performance and clips circulated widely on TikTok by midnight.
6
Copying a press release word for word
A press release is a PR document. Rewrite it. Pull the facts, get an independent quote where possible, add context. The story must read like journalism — not marketing.
